Ultralight Twin-Motor RC Airplane

An Ultralight Airframe with Integrated Micro-Electronics

Constructed from foam board, balsa wood, and paper, this ultralight radio-controlled airplane has a gross weight of under 180 grams.

This plane utilises components sourced from a recycled drone. The constraints of the low-torque motors are reflected in the lightweight design through material choice and minimalist construction. Additionally, a dual-propeller design was conceptualised to retain maneouverability without the need of weight expenses in a moveable rudder, and the wings provide necessary lift to support flight without the need of ailerons.

The final design of this airplane was reached through a process of reiterative prototyping. Since this project was heavily constrained by the low power of the motors, 3 prototypes were studied before the final design, to perfect the weight balance and aerodynamics such that true flight could be supported.

Inline-4 Engine Crankshaft and Piston Assembly

From a set of isometric drawings sourced online, I modelled the individual components, and assembled them into a crankshaft and piston assembly.

This project was significantly more rigorous than my previous work, and further familiarised me with using SolidWorks to for more complex asssemblies.

With a system of mates and the motion study function within SolidWorks, I was also able to define the motion of the assembly and simulate its real-world motion.
